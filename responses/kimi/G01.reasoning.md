The question asks: identify potential problems with this backtest.

This is a quantitative finance question about backtest biases and methodological flaws. Let me systematically identify the issues:

1. **Survivorship bias (幸存者偏差)**: "以当前沪深300成分股为股票池" — using CURRENT CSI 300 constituents to backtest 2015-2025. This is a classic survivorship bias / look-ahead bias. Stocks that were removed from the index (due to poor performance, delisting, etc.) are excluded from the pool. The current constituents are the "survivors" — companies that performed well enough to remain in or be added to the index. This inflates backtest returns. The correct approach: use historical constituents at each point in time (point-in-time data).

2. **Look-ahead bias (前视偏差/未来函数)**: "每天用当日收盘价计算信号，并以当日收盘价成交" — computing signals using today's closing price AND trading at today's closing price. This is impossible in practice: you can't know the closing price until the market closes, at which point you can no longer trade at that price. This is a look-ahead bias. Realistic approach: compute signal at close, trade at next day's open (or next day's close, or VWAP, etc.). Unless the signal uses only data available before the close (e.g., intraday data before 14:57), you cannot trade at the same close.

3. **Overfitting / data mining bias (过拟合/数据挖掘偏差/多重检验问题)**: Testing 200 parameter combinations and selecting the best one — this is selection bias / multiple testing problem. The best of 200 in-sample results is likely overfit; out-of-sample performance will likely be much worse. Need: out-of-sample testing, cross-validation (walk-forward), deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey et al.), Bonferroni corrections, White's reality check, etc. Also the reported Sharpe of 3.0 is suspiciously high, which is consistent with overfitting. With 200 trials, even random strategies will produce some with high Sharpe by chance.

4. **No transaction costs (未计交易成本)**: A-share costs include commission (~0.02-0.03%), stamp duty (0.05-0.1% on sells, was 0.1%, reduced to 0.05% in 2023, then halved... actually stamp duty: 0.1% before Aug 2023, 0.05% after), slippage, market impact. If the strategy trades frequently (daily signals!), costs could consume a large portion of returns. Daily signal generation suggests potentially high turnover. For a strategy with 35% annualized return but high turnover, costs matter enormously.

5. **Other potential issues**:
   - **Suspicious performance**: 35% annualized return with Sharpe 3.0 over 10 years is extremely rare — better than virtually all famous hedge funds. This itself is a red flag suggesting biases.
   - **T+1 rule in A-shares**: You can't sell same-day bought shares; also trading at close may face practical constraints.
   - **Limit up/down (涨跌停)**: Can't actually trade at limit prices; backtest may assume fills at prices where real trading is impossible (e.g., buying at limit-up close).
   - **Liquidity/market impact**: Assuming all trades fill at close without impact; if strategy capacity is large, this matters. Also 集合竞价 (closing auction) mechanics.
   - **Dividends/adjustments**: Need to use adjusted prices (复权) properly; using forward-adjusted prices incorrectly can introduce issues; but mainly ensure dividend handling.
   - **ST stocks, suspended stocks (停牌)**: Can't trade during suspension; 2015 had massive suspensions during the crash. Backtest may ignore these.
   - **IPO / newly listed stocks**: CSI 300 additions.
   - **Benchmark comparison**: Report doesn't mention benchmark or excess return, beta exposure — the period 2015-2025 includes bull and bear markets.
   - **Delisting**: stocks delisted entirely would be missing.
   - **Signal calculation and execution timing**: Also settlement issues.
