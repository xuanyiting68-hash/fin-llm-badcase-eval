The user is asking in Chinese: "The Federal Reserve announced a 25 basis point rate hike, will gold prices definitely fall? Please explain the transmission logic."

This is a finance/economics question about the relationship between Fed rate hikes and gold prices. The question contains a common misconception — that rate hikes *definitely* cause gold prices to fall. I should address this carefully.

Let me think about the economics here:

**The traditional transmission logic (why rate hikes tend to pressure gold):**

1. **Opportunity cost channel**: Gold is a non-yielding asset (zero coupon). When the Fed raises rates, yields on Treasury bonds, deposits, and money market instruments rise. The opportunity cost of holding gold increases, so investors shift toward interest-bearing assets → downward pressure on gold.

2. **Dollar channel**: Rate hikes tend to strengthen the dollar (capital flows into USD assets seeking higher returns, interest rate parity). Since gold is priced in dollars internationally, a stronger dollar makes gold more expensive for non-USD buyers, reducing demand → downward pressure. Also, there's typically an inverse correlation between DXY and gold.

3. **Real interest rate channel**: This is the most important one. Gold's key driver is real yields (nominal yield minus inflation expectations). Real rates = nominal rates − inflation expectations. Higher nominal rates, if inflation expectations stay constant, raise real rates → gold falls. The 10-year TIPS yield has a strong negative correlation with gold.

4. **Inflation expectations channel**: Rate hikes signal anti-inflation commitment, which can lower inflation expectations → reduces gold's appeal as an inflation hedge.

**Why "definitely falls" is wrong:**

1. **Real rates matter, not nominal rates**: If inflation expectations rise faster than nominal rates (e.g., the hike is seen as behind the curve), real rates can actually *fall*, which is bullish for gold. Example: 1970s stagflation — Fed hiking but gold soared because inflation ran hotter.

2. **Expectation vs. realization (buy rumor, sell news / priced in)**: Markets are forward-looking. A 25bp hike that's fully anticipated is already priced in. Gold often rallies *after* the hike announcement ("sell the rumor, buy the news"). Examples: 
   - December 2015: first hike in the cycle, gold bottomed right around then and rallied in early 2016.
   - March 2022: first hike of that cycle, gold actually rose initially (though Ukraine war was a factor).
   - Actually, looking at the 2015-2018 hiking cycle, gold often rose after hikes because they were well telegraphed.
   - 2022-2023: aggressive hikes, gold initially fell from ~$2070 (March 2022) to ~$1618 (Nov 2022), but then recovered strongly even as hikes continued.

3. **Hawkish vs. dovish context**: What matters is the hike relative to expectations plus forward guidance (dot plot, Powell's press conference). A 25bp hike with dovish guidance can be net bullish for gold; a 25bp hike with hawkish surprises can be bearish.

4. **Risk aversion/safe-haven effects**: If the hike triggers recession fears or financial instability (e.g., March 2023 SVB crisis), gold can rise on safe-haven demand despite hikes.

5. **Central bank demand and de-dollarization**: In recent years (2022-2024), central bank gold purchases (China, etc.) have decoupled gold from real rates to some extent. Gold hit all-time highs in 2024 even with high real rates.

6. **The dollar doesn't always strengthen**: If other central banks (ECB, BOJ) are hiking more aggressively or catching up, rate differentials may not widen, so the dollar may not strengthen.

7. **Starting conditions**: If the hike cycle is near its end, markets anticipate cuts, so gold may rise on the "last hike" narrative.

**Historical examples to cite:**
- Dec 2015 first hike: gold bottomed and then rallied ~30% into mid-2016.
- 2004-2006 hiking cycle: gold rose from ~$400 to ~$700+ during the entire cycle (17 consecutive 25bp hikes).
- 2022: aggressive hikes pushed gold down initially, but by late 2022 gold recovered.
- 1970s: Volcker era aside, during Burns/Miller era hikes lagging inflation, gold soared.

**传导逻辑（教科书式的利空路径）**:

1. 机会成本渠道：黄金本身不生息（零息资产）。加息→美债收益率、存款利率上升→持有黄金的机会成本上升→资金从黄金流向生息资产→金价承压。

2. 美元渠道：加息→美元资产回报率提高→资本流入美元→美元指数走强→以美元计价的黄金对非美货币持有者变贵→需求下降→金价承压。金价与美元指数通常负相关。

3. 实际利率渠道（核心）：金价与实际利率（名义利率−通胀预期）高度负相关，常用10年期TIPS收益率衡量。加息若推高实际利率→金价下跌。

4. 抗通胀需求渠道：加息传递抗通胀决心→通胀预期降温→黄金抗通胀的吸引力下降。

**为什么不一定跌（反例机制）**:

1. 预期已提前消化：市场定价是前瞻性的。如果25个基点早已被充分预期（priced in），落地时反而可能"利空出尽"反弹。"买预期、卖事实"反过来就是"卖预期、买事实"。

2. 关键看实际利率而非名义利率：如果通胀预期上升快于名义利率（市场认为美联储落后于曲线），实际利率反而下降→金价上涨。1970s滞胀时期就是例子。
