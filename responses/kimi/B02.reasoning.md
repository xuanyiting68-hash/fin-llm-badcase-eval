This is a finance/economics question. Let me think through this carefully.

The conventional view: Gold doesn't pay interest or dividends, so its opportunity cost is the real interest rate. When real yields rise, holding gold becomes less attractive, so gold prices typically fall. There's a well-documented negative correlation between gold prices and real yields (especially 10-year TIPS yields).

But the question is about NOMINAL yields, not real yields. This is a crucial distinction!

Nominal yield = real yield + inflation expectations (approximately, via Fisher equation)

So if nominal yields rise because inflation expectations rise faster than real yields, then real yields could actually FALL while nominal yields rise. In that case, gold rising is perfectly consistent with the traditional framework.

Key explanations:

1. **Real yields vs nominal yields distinction**: The key driver of gold is real interest rates (opportunity cost of holding a zero-yield asset). If nominal yields rise due to rising inflation expectations (breakeven inflation rising), real yields may fall or stay flat. Gold is an inflation hedge, so rising inflation expectations boost gold. This is the most fundamental explanation.

2. **Inflation expectations rising faster than nominal yields**: Even if real yields rise slightly, if inflation expectations surge, gold as inflation hedge may rise.

3. **Safe-haven demand / risk aversion**: If yields are rising because of fiscal concerns, debt sustainability worries (e.g., concerns about US fiscal deficit leading to term premium rising), this could simultaneously push nominal yields up (term premium) and gold up (loss of confidence in fiat/US debt). This is the "debasement trade" narrative.

4. **Central bank purchases**: Structural demand shift - central banks (China, India, Turkey, Poland, etc.) buying gold for reserve diversification, de-dollarization. This can decouple gold from real yields. This happened notably 2022-2024 when real yields rose sharply but gold stayed strong or rose.

5. **Geopolitical risk**: Wars, sanctions (freezing of Russian reserves), etc. increase gold demand regardless of rates.

6. **Different segments of the yield story**: Nominal yields can rise due to:
   - Higher real yields (bad for gold, ceteris paribus)
   - Higher inflation expectations (good for gold)
   - Higher term premium (ambiguous - if due to fiscal risk, could be good for gold)

Decomposition matters.

7. **Correlation isn't deterministic**: The gold-real yield relationship is a tendency, not a law. Other factors dominate at times: dollar movements, ETF flows, jewelry demand, momentum/CTA buying, etc.

8. **Dollar channel**: Gold is priced in dollars. If the dollar is weak, gold can rise even with rising yields. Though usually rising US yields support the dollar... but not always (e.g., if yields rise due to fiscal risk, dollar may fall - "US exceptionalism unwind").

9. **Expectations and front-running**: Markets may expect future rate cuts or expect the Fed to fall behind the curve (real rates to fall), so gold rises in anticipation.

10. **Time horizon / lag issues**: The relationship may hold over long horizons but break down in short windows.

Historical examples:
- 1970s: nominal yields rose but inflation rose faster; real rates were negative; gold soared.
- 2022-2024: Real yields rose significantly (10y TIPS from -1% to ~2%), yet gold rose from ~$1,800 to $2,700+. Explanation: central bank buying (record purchases after Russian reserves frozen), geopolitical hedging, fiscal concerns.
- 2007-2008, 2020: various episodes.

所以名义收益率上升可以来自完全不同的驱动因素，对黄金的含义截然不同：

**情形一：通胀预期驱动（最常见）**
