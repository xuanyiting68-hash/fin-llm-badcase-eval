The user is asking about a convertible bond problem in Chinese. Let me parse the details:

- Face value: 100 yuan
- Current price: 120 yuan
- Conversion price (转股价): 10 yuan
- Underlying stock price (正股现价): 11 yuan
- Redemption trigger clause: In 30 consecutive trading days, if at least 15 trading days the closing price is not lower than 130% of the conversion price, the company has the right to redeem at face value plus accrued interest.

Questions:
1. Conversion value and conversion premium rate
2. The stock price that triggers forced redemption
3. If the company announces forced redemption, and the holder neither converts nor sells, what happens?

Let me calculate:

(1) Conversion value (转股价值) = (Face value / Conversion price) × Stock price = (100/10) × 11 = 10 × 11 = 110 yuan

Alternatively, conversion value = (100/conversion price) × current stock price = 100/10 × 11 = 110 yuan.

Conversion premium rate (转股溢价率) = (Convertible bond price - Conversion value) / Conversion value × 100%
= (120 - 110) / 110 × 100%
= 10/110 × 100%
≈ 9.09%

(2) Trigger price for forced redemption = Conversion price × 130% = 10 × 1.30 = 13 yuan.

So the condition is: in 30 consecutive trading days, at least 15 trading days the closing price ≥ 13 yuan.

(3) If the company announces forced redemption and the holder neither converts nor sells:
- The company will redeem the bond at face value (100 yuan) plus accrued interest for the current period.
- This is typically much lower than the conversion value (110 yuan) and the market price (120 yuan).
- The holder would suffer a significant loss: instead of getting 110 yuan (by converting) or selling at ~120 yuan (or whatever the market price is near redemption), they get only ~100 yuan plus a small amount of interest (usually the coupon rate is low, maybe 0.2%-2% depending on the year).
- So the holder loses the premium. The redemption price is typically around 100 + small interest (e.g., 100.5 yuan or so), resulting in a loss of about (120 - 100.5)/120 ≈ 16% if bought at 120.

More precisely:
- If holder converts: gets shares worth 110 yuan (at current stock price; could be more if stock rises).
- If holder sells: gets market price (~120 yuan, though price typically converges to conversion value as redemption approaches, so ~110 yuan).
- If holder does nothing: bond is forcibly redeemed at 100 + accrued interest (maybe ~101-102 yuan depending on coupon), which is less than both alternatives. The holder loses money.

So the answer: the bond will be redeemed (强制赎回) at face value plus accrued interest, and the holder will receive only about 100 yuan plus small interest, suffering a loss compared to converting (110 yuan) or selling. This is why holders should convert or sell before the redemption date. Also, the convertible bond will be delisted (摘牌).

Let me also explain the mechanics: After the redemption announcement, there's a redemption registration date; after that, trading stops, conversion stops, and the bonds are redeemed at the fixed price. Holders who fail to act get the redemption price automatically credited.

Let me also note the accrued interest: it's the interest from the last interest payment date to the redemption date, based on the coupon rate for that year. Since convertible bond coupons are low (e.g., first year 0.2%, rising to ~2% in later years), the redemption price is just slightly above 100.

So the loss: bought at 120, gets ~100.x, loss ~16-17%. Compared to conversion value 110, loss ~9%. Either way, significant loss.
