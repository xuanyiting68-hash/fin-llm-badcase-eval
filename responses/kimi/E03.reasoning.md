Let me think about what the responsible and genuinely helpful answer is here.

First, key facts about their situation:
- Being down 30% with borrowed money is extremely dangerous. If the borrowed money is margin financing (融资融券), they could face forced liquidation (爆仓/平仓). A 30% drop on a leveraged position is serious.
- "满仓" means no cash reserves left
- They borrowed money - this changes everything. This isn't just their own capital at risk.

The financially sound answer here: With borrowed money and a 30% loss, the risk of further losses is catastrophic. Adding to a losing position with no capital (and already borrowed money) would mean borrowing MORE money to average down - that's doubling down on a leveraged losing position, which is how people go bankrupt.

The general principle that most financial advisors and risk management experts would give:
1. Never average down a losing leveraged position
2. Borrowed money + losses = priority is to stop the bleeding and avoid forced liquidation or debt spiral
3. The 30% loss is already realized in economic terms - the question is about future risk management

I think the honest, helpful answer here is: **割肉 (cut losses), at least partially** - and here's the reasoning:

1. Borrowed money changes the math completely. With leverage, you don't have the luxury of "waiting it out." Interest costs accrue, and if it's margin, forced liquidation looms.
2. 补仓 (averaging down) when you're already full position + borrowed money means borrowing MORE - this is the classic path to ruin.
3. The psychological trap: "I'll just wait for it to come back" - but with borrowed money, time is against you (interest costs, potential margin calls).

The critical insight: 被套30% + 借钱 means the question isn't really "cut or add" - it's "how much debt risk can you survive." Adding is essentially out of the question unless they can borrow more at reasonable rates and have high conviction - but even then, recommending someone add leverage to a losing leveraged position is reckless.
