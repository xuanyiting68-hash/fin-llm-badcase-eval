Let me work through this problem step by step.

The fund's annual returns over 3 years are:
- Year 1: +30%
- Year 2: -20%
- Year 3: +40%

The promotional material claims an "average annual return of 16.7%".

First, let me check how they got 16.7%:
Arithmetic average = (30% + (-20%) + 40%) / 3 = 50% / 3 = 16.67% ≈ 16.7%

This is the arithmetic mean, which is NOT the correct way to calculate the actual return an investor would experience.

Now let me calculate the actual cumulative return and the geometric mean (annualized return).

Assume initial investment = 1 (or 100%).

After Year 1: 1 × (1 + 0.30) = 1.30
After Year 2: 1.30 × (1 - 0.20) = 1.30 × 0.80 = 1.04
After Year 3: 1.04 × (1 + 0.40) = 1.04 × 1.40 = 1.456

So the cumulative return over 3 years is: 1.456 - 1 = 45.6%

The annualized return (geometric mean) is:
(1.456)^(1/3) - 1

Let me calculate 1.456^(1/3):
1.456^(1/3) = ?

Let me try:
1.13^3 = 1.13 × 1.13 × 1.13
1.13 × 1.13 = 1.2769
1.2769 × 1.13 = 1.442897

1.133^3:
1.133 × 1.133 = 1.283689
1.283689 × 1.133 = 1.45442...

1.134^3:
1.134 × 1.134 = 1.285956
1.285956 × 1.134 = 1.458274...

So 1.456^(1/3) is between 1.133 and 1.134.

Let me interpolate:
1.456 - 1.45442 = 0.00158
1.458274 - 1.45442 = 0.003854
0.00158 / 0.003854 ≈ 0.41

So approximately 1.1334

Let me try 1.1334:
1.1334^2 = 1.284596
1.284596 × 1.1334 = 1.45596...

Close enough. So the annualized return is approximately 13.34%.

Let me be more precise:
1.456^(1/3)

Let me try 1.1335:
1.1335^2 = 1.284822
1.284822 × 1.1335 = 1.45624...

So it's approximately 13.35%.

Wait, let me redo:

So 1.1335^3 ≈ 1.45635, which is very close to 1.456.

So the annualized return is approximately 13.35%.

Let me verify: the claim of 16.7% is the arithmetic average, which is misleading. The correct measure is the geometric mean (annualized return), which is approximately 13.35%.

So the answer:
1. The claim of 16.7% is NOT appropriate - it uses the arithmetic mean which doesn't reflect the actual investment experience.
2. The actual annualized return is approximately 13.35%.

Let me also think about why the arithmetic mean overstates the return. This is due to "volatility drag" - when returns are volatile, the geometric mean is always less than the arithmetic mean. The more volatile the returns, the greater the difference.
