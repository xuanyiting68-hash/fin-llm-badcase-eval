Let me work through this step-by-step.

Given:
- Period: 5 years (60 months)
- Monthly Rank IC mean: 0.03
- IC standard deviation: 0.10
- Percentage of months with positive IC: 58%

**Step 1: Calculate Monthly ICIR**

ICIR (Information Coefficient Information Ratio) = IC均值 / IC标准差

Monthly ICIR = 0.03 / 0.10 = 0.3

**Step 2: Annualize ICIR**

按 √12 约定年化：

Annualized ICIR = Monthly ICIR × √12 = 0.3 × √12 = 0.3 × 3.4641 ≈ 1.039

**Step 3: Evaluate whether the factor is effective**

Common criteria for factor effectiveness:
1. **ICIR**: Generally, annualized ICIR > 0.5 is considered acceptable, > 1.0 is considered good. Here, annualized ICIR ≈ 1.04, which is good.

2. **IC mean**: Monthly IC of 0.03 is generally considered meaningful (typically > 0.02 or 0.03 is considered useful).

3. **IC positive ratio**: 58% is above 50%, which is a positive sign. Generally > 55% is considered decent.

4. **Statistical significance**: We can also check the t-statistic.
   - t = IC均值 / (IC标准差 / √n) = 0.03 / (0.10 / √60) = 0.03 / (0.10 / 7.746) = 0.03 / 0.01291 = 2.324
   - With 59 degrees of freedom, t ≈ 2.32 is significant at the 5% level (critical value ≈ 2.00), but not at the 1% level (critical value ≈ 2.66).

5. **Stability**: IC standard deviation of 0.10 relative to IC mean of 0.03 is quite high, meaning the factor is somewhat noisy, but the ICIR accounts for this.

Let me also think about the t-statistic more carefully:
- The t-test for whether the mean IC is significantly different from zero:
- t = (IC_mean - 0) / (IC_std / √n) = 0.03 / (0.10 / √60)
- √60 = 7.746
- 0.10 / 7.746 = 0.01291

This is significant at 5% level (two-tailed critical value for df=59 is approximately 2.001).

So the factor appears to be statistically significant and practically meaningful.
